## 2. Four white-space opportunities

### WS-1 — Documents: *People want one document task done once, locally and anonymously; every consumer PDF tool sells a 7-day trial that becomes an annual subscription.*

**Demand proof**
- **95,289 GitHub stars** on Stirling-PDF ("#1 PDF Application on GitHub"); the top of the funnel for "I don't want to upload my file to your cloud" is enormous — [api.github.com](https://api.github.com/repos/Stirling-Tools/Stirling-PDF).
- A 1,303-point HN thread on **PDF ingestion** shows the failure mode people actually care about is extraction fidelity, not conversion chrome — [HN 42952605](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42952605).

**Why current products are weak**
- iLovePDF: a one-time user is billed **$48/yr**, and gets billed **$48 again the next year without warning** — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ac91bc421762596d0bcfb9e), [pricing](https://www.ilovepdf.com/pricing).
- Smallpdf: 7-day trial → **annual** charge; "rigid, scripted replies" on refund — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/reviews/6adee4418f7ed6123c059a3f).
- Adobe charges an **early-termination fee of up to half the remaining annual commitment** for not wanting Acrobat anymore — [adobe.com/acrobat/pricing](https://www.adobe.com/acrobat/pricing.html).
- Sejda is the only incumbent that admits the need (**$5 week pass**) — and still funnels to seats — [sejda.com/pricing](https://www.sejda.com/pricing).

**The wedge:** local/in-browser processing, no account, **$5 one-off** (or 3 free tasks/day), one narrow high-fidelity operation done better than anyone (e.g. scanned PDF → clean structured table with confidence flags). Selling point is arithmetic: **$5 once vs $48/yr.**

### WS-2 — Job search: *People want one honest, job-specific ATS match check; every tool sells a $25.95–$29.95-per-4-weeks subscription and gates the download behind it.*

**Demand proof**
- **1,032-point / 433-comment** HN story: "HackerRank open sourced its ATS. My resume scored 90/100. Oh wait 74. No – 88" — the top comments are people arguing about score reproducibility and their own callback rate — [HN 48713832](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48713832).

**Why current products are weak**
- Zety: **$1.95 trial → $25.95 every 4 weeks** (≈$337/yr); users report charges for accounts the company says don't exist — [pricing](https://www.zety.com/pricing), [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ad4bfbb92a698f9b3e52b85).
- Resume.io: **$2.95 trial → $29.95 every 4 weeks**; the paywall appears exactly at PDF download — [pricing](https://resume.io/pricing), [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ad191e3be54e9f8f93bc983).
- Jobscan: trial converts to paid unexpectedly per reviewers, and the score itself is contested — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/reviews/6abc3133b5823a64902a5797). *(Jobscan's own pricing page did not render for me → **price UNVERIFIED**.)*

**The wedge:** **free, no-signup score** against one pasted job description, with the *evidence* shown (which keyword, which line, which requirement missed), then **$9 one-off** for the full rewrite report. No account. No subscription. Reproducible scoring (deterministic keyword/requirements diff first, LLM only for phrasing) is the trust hook that directly answers the HN critique.

### WS-3 — Spreadsheets: *People want to clean, compare or reconcile one messy spreadsheet in the browser with a visible audit trail; every tool is a Java desktop power tool, a $99.99 Windows app, an enterprise-only Excel feature, or a $12-per-run niche site.*

**Demand proof**
- **708-point HN thread "A love letter to the CSV format"** — the top comments are practitioners describing garbage CSVs emitted by "very expensive, very complicated" software and quoting/escaping failures — [HN 43484382](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43484382).
- Repeated HN launches in exactly this slot: **TextQuery** (query CSV/JSON/XLSX with SQL, 163 pts — [HN 43897129](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43897129)), **Visprex** (in-browser CSV visualisation, 194 pts — [HN 42096837](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42096837)). Launches keep recurring → demand keeps re-asserting itself.
- **OpenRefine: 12,081★ but 727 open issues** — a beloved tool with no modern consumer path — [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/OpenRefine/OpenRefine).

**Why current products are weak**
- **xlCompare: $99.99 lifetime, Windows desktop only** — [order page](https://xlcompare.com/order.html).
- Microsoft's own "Spreadsheet Compare" is unfindable for normal users — recurring Microsoft Q&A thread "How can I access Excel compare sheet functionality?" — [learn.microsoft.com](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5300828/how-can-i-access-excel-compare-sheet-functionality).
- **ReconcileOnline** already monetises reconciliation at **$12/run** (5 runs $49, 40 runs at $6) — proof of willingness-to-pay, and proof the category is served by exactly one thin indie site — [reconcileonline.com/pricing](https://reconcileonline.com/pricing).

**The wedge:** *"Two files in → one diff out, with a reason for every mismatch."* Browser-only, **auditable rule log** ("row 4,182 matched on invoice #, differed on amount by 0.02, classified as rounding"), shareable read-only diff link (the viral artifact), **$12 per reconciliation or $19/mo** — priced under xlCompare's licence and above ReconcileOnline's per-run ceiling.

### WS-4 — Meeting notes: *People want a private, local transcript they own; every AI notetaker puts a bot in the call, ships audio to a cloud, and charges per seat with a minimum.*

**Demand proof**
- **Hyprnote (YC S25): 270 pts / 180 comments** on launch, **9,621★** on GitHub, explicitly "Open source Granola AI Alternative" — [HN 44725306](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44725306), [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/fastrepl/hyprnote).
- **Trace: 205 pts** — "Show HN: Trace – Offline Mac meeting transcripts you can flag mid-call", and a follow-up re-launch after HN feedback — [HN 48521236](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48521236).

**Why current products are weak**
- Fireflies: Mac app auto-joins every call and won't stop — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a909add22e9e2c2fc8b0963); support is an AI bot that loses billing context — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ac66b228f92b6cd6e144192).
- Fathom: **2-user minimum at $19/user** for a solo consultant — [fathom.video/pricing](https://fathom.video/pricing).
- Otter: free tier dies mid-meeting at 300 min/user — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ad01380f037dc5d7bba753f), [otter.ai/pricing](https://otter.ai/pricing).
- Granola is **Mac-only** and Business is $14/user — [granola.ai/pricing](https://www.granola.ai/pricing).

**Verdict: real white space, *wrong* 5-day build** — see §3.

## 3. Most defensible for a 5-day build

**Winner: WS-3 (spreadsheet clean/compare/reconcile).** Runner-up: WS-2.

| Criterion | WS-1 PDF | WS-2 Resume | **WS-3 Spreadsheets** | WS-4 Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buildable in 5 days by a small team | Medium — PDF parsing/rendering edge cases | **High** — text + one LLM call | **High** — deterministic diff; optionally one LLM call for "why" | ✗ Needs native audio capture, OS permissions, installers |
| Free/strong incumbent risk | **High** — Stirling is free at 95k★ | High — Jobscan/Teal + ChatGPT | **Low** — incumbents are $99.99 desktop, enterprise-only Excel, or a single indie site | High — Hyprnote OSS + 4 funded SaaS |
| Quality moat (hard to clone in a weekend) | Low — wrappers abound | Low — prompter's game | **Medium-high** — matching heuristics, type coercion, fuzzy keys, rounding tolerance; quality shows immediately | Medium — model+audio stack |
| Willingness to pay proven | Yes (everyone already pays) | Yes ($26–30/4wk) | **Yes — $99.99 licence, $12/run** | Yes ($14–19/seat) |
| Distribution surface | Very crowded SEO | Very crowded, low-trust | **Wide long tail** ("compare two excel files", "vlookup alternative", "dedupe 40k rows") | Crowded, desktop-gated |
| Why now | AI Overviews eat "compress pdf" | Trust collapse in resume-scam category | AI makes "explain the mismatch" cheap; nobody has done it | Local-model privacy wave |

**Why WS-3 wins:** the core is *deterministic*, so a 5-day build can be genuinely correct while every competitor's LLM-wrapper is fuzzy. Failure modes are self-evident to the user (wrong match = visible), which means refunds are low and word-of-mouth is high. And the category has an incumbent price anchor (**$99.99** Windows licence / **$12 per run**) that a $12–19 web product undercuts without a price war — nobody in this category is free.

**5-day scope (be strict):**
- **Day 1–2:** upload two files (CSV/XLSX), auto-key detection, exact + fuzzy row matching, tolerance for numeric/date formatting.
- **Day 3:** the diff UI — Added / Missing / Changed, cell-level, with a plain-language reason per row.
- **Day 4:** audit log export (CSV + PDF), shareable read-only diff link (requires no login to *view* — this is the loop).
- **Day 5:** pricing ($12/run, $19/mo), Stripe, 3 free diffs/day, one landing page with 6 "how to" pages.
- **Explicitly out of scope:** live sync, Google Sheets API, collaborative editing, more than 2 files.

**Honest risk:** a brand-new domain has no traffic, and this wedge is a *pull* product — it needs search intent to exist, and search intent here is **UNVERIFIED** (I could not access a keyword-volume source). If the team cannot rank or find a community where reconciliation is discussed weekly, WS-3 dies of invisibility, not of competition. That is the single kill-condition.

## 4. Distribution reality check (first 100 users, no paid ads)

### The 2026 baseline
- **Google is no longer a reliable cold-start channel.** In Pew's browsing-panel study, **58% of users hit an AI summary** in a sampled month of searches, and users clicked result links **less often** on pages with an AI summary; cited sources were "very rarely" clicked — [Pew Research, 22 Jul 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). An AI summary answers "how do I compare two Excel files" *without* sending the user to a tool.
- **Launching is not a channel anymore.** Caleb Porzio, Dec 2025: *"It's not what it was 10 years ago. You can't just throw something on Product Hunt and get zillions of views… unless your thing is incredibly groundbreaking, launching won't work. You have to ship every day… and do it kind of silently."* — [Notes On Work, "Is launching dead?"](https://www.podscan.fm/podcasts/notes-on-work-by-caleb-porzio/episodes/is-launching-dead)
- **A documented cold-start failure with numbers (May 2026):** a founder shipped an MVP in 10 days. Result: *"0 paying customers · ~10 cold DMs sent on LinkedIn · 2 replies · 0 signups from those replies · Submitted to HN yesterday, got flagged, 30–40 visitors, 0 signups."* — [Indie Hackers post](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-built-a-saas-in-10-days-3-weeks-live-0-paying-customers-heres-the-data-7cb6b6accf)

### Per-opportunity: how the first 100 users actually arrive

- **WS-1 (PDF):** Excel/Workspace-style marketplace is unavailable; the honest path is *free browser tool + 6 comparison pages* ("Smallpdf alternative without a subscription"), ranked over months, plus a **Reddit/HN "I got charged $48 for one merge"** narrative post. Expect **slow**; the category's SEO is owned by incumbents with decade-old domains. Weakest cold-start of the four.
- **WS-2 (Resume):** highest-intent search volume in the set, but also the most spam-saturated and lowest-trust; a new domain is assumed to be a scam *because the incumbents are*. Viable only as a **free tool with no signup** ("paste job description, get score in 10 seconds") promoted in job-seeker communities. The no-signup free score *is* the marketing.
- **WS-3 (Spreadsheets):** best cold-start of the four. Three live paths: (a) **Excel/Google Sheets add-in listing**, (b) long-tail pages for error-shaped queries, (c) **community answers** where someone asks "how do I find what changed between two exports" — you answer with the tool and a shared diff link. The shareable read-only diff link is the only genuine viral loop in the set.
- **WS-4 (Notes):** distribution is *developer-native* (GitHub + HN) and that path is already occupied by Hyprnote; a closed-source newcomer has no angle here.

### What is genuinely dead in 2026
- **Paid-ads-free launch spikes** (PH) — dead as an acquisition channel; the network requirement predates you.
- **Generic "AI wrapper" positioning** — the HN commentariat now openly calls out fabricated social proof and "vibecoded GPT wrappers" claiming SOC2 — [HN 44725306 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44725306). Any trust badge you can't substantiate will be the story instead of your product.
- **Head-term SEO** — dead against incumbents; Pew's data explains why even winning the ranking yields fewer clicks.

## 5. Confidence & gaps

**Solid (fetched, directly quoted):** all pricing in §1 except Smallpdf/Otter/Descript plan amounts; Adobe cancellation-fee language; Zety and Resume.io trial→renewal numbers; Fathom 2-user minimum; Granola $14 + Mac-only; Surfer four tiers; Bonsai three tiers; OpusClip three tiers; xlCompare $99.99; ReconcileOnline per-run pricing; GitHub star counts; all HN point counts; Pew findings; the IH 0-customer numbers.

**UNVERIFIED (explicitly not invented):**
- Smallpdf, Otter, Descript **plan dollar amounts** (client-rendered, not in page text or JSON).
- **Jobscan** pricing; **Gigasheet** and **Flatfile** pricing (Gigasheet's /pricing now serves enterprise data-licensing copy, not the spreadsheet tool).
- **FreshBooks** subscription price (only the **1% ACH fee** complaint was verified — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ae265eef3ce3a6c584295f1)).
- **Search volume** for every white-space query. This is the biggest unknown and the reason WS-3 carries a distribution kill-condition rather than a market kill-condition.
- **Reddit, G2, Product Hunt** content (all bot-walled). Product Hunt's decline is stated as consensus only.
- Any statement about which specific indie tool "died and why" — I found postmortems I could not authenticate, so none are cited.

**Deliberate omission:** I did not include an invoice/expense white space. The evidence I could verify (FreshBooks' 1% ACH fee, Bonsai's bundling) shows *pricing resentment*, not a missing capability — too thin to claim white space honestly. If the team wants a fifth candidate, that is the one to research next, not to build on.
